Only the identifiers you type are queried. The dashboard stores reports under scans/ on this machine and nowhere else.

What gets checked

  • Public profile pages — a curated set of sites, each with a declared signature (404 semantics, a stable "no such user" phrase, or a sign-in wall).
  • Public JSON APIs — GitHub, GitLab, Codeberg, Docker Hub, Keybase, Bluesky, Mastodon, Chess.com, Lichess, Codeforces, npm, dev.to, Gravatar, RDAP and more.
  • Research records — OpenAlex, arXiv, PubMed, ORCID and Wikipedia, always labelled low confidence because names are not unique.

What it will never do

  • No people-search brokers or reverse-lookup services.
  • No downloading, scraping or bundling of leaked databases — a local corpus is something you supply.
  • No login walls, CAPTCHAs or anti-bot controls circumvented.
  • No phone reverse lookups, addresses or relatives — out of scope by design.
  • No queries about anyone but the identifiers you supply.

Confidence

CONFIRMED structured API record · HIGH unambiguous site signal · MEDIUM signature heuristic · LOW weak heuristic or same-name candidate

Ask about your own scans

Answers come from a model running on this machine — a GGUF file loaded with llama-cpp-python, an Ollama daemon on loopback, or (with neither installed) plain retrieval over the report. Nothing about your footprint is sent to a company to be summarised, the conversation is never written to disk, and every answer cites the context ids it used. Ids it invented are removed before you see them.

Verify which findings are really you optional

A scan can tell that a page exists; it cannot tell whether the account is yours. Tick the box and the local model will give a second opinion on each finding — MINE, NOT_MINE or UNSURE. This never runs on its own: not during a scan, not during a breach check, not when you ask a question. A verdict is an opinion kept beside the measured confidence — it cannot raise it — and it is not proof of ownership. Check the URL before you act on one.
